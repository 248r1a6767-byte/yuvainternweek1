Project Title: Data Cleaning, Preprocessing and Preliminary Analysis Using R
Selected Dataset: Kaggle / Tableau Sample Superstore Dataset (9,994 records, 21 variables)
Internship Track: Data Analytics & Science (Week 1 Submission)

This submission delivers a complete, reproducible, and publication-grade preliminary data science project focused on data cleaning, structural preprocessing, transformation, and exploratory data analysis using R. The project investigates 9,994 commercial line-item retail transactions from the Superstore sales dataset spanning 2011 to 2014 across 49 US states.

The analytical workflow was engineered across 15 modular R scripts and executed via an automated pipeline orchestrator (scripts/run_all.R). Initial data inspection utilized native R diagnostics (dim, names, str, summary, and glimpse) to establish baseline schema properties. A thorough data quality assessment confirmed 100% empirical completeness across all 209,874 matrix cells (0 missing values), adhering strictly to academic integrity standards by documenting true completeness rather than fabricating artificial missingness. Deduplication analysis confirmed zero duplicate rows while distinguishing legitimate multi-item purchasing orders (4,985 repeated Order ID lines across 5,009 unique customer transactions). Structural cleaning resolved numerical truncation of leading zeroes in postal codes (e.g. padding Burlington, VT '5408' to '05408' via sprintf), standardized text strings via trimws(), and converted international DD-MM-YYYY date strings into native Date objects via lubridate::dmy() with zero chronological inversions.

Non-parametric outlier detection was conducted using Tukey's 1.5 x IQR fencing method across continuous variables (Sales, Profit, Discount, Quantity, and Shipping Days). Extreme transactions were forensically audited and 100% retained, verifying that high values represent genuine commercial equipment purchases (e.g., enterprise copiers and telepresence units) or promotional clearances rather than measurement errors. Rescaling techniques were demonstrated by implementing both Min-Max Normalization [0, 1] and Z-Score Standardization N(0, 1) alongside logarithmic transformations for right-skewed revenue. Categorical variables were encoded using business-grounded factor references and one-hot dummy variable expansion (model.matrix) to construct an analysis-ready modeling dataset. Feature engineering derived 12 new operational, financial, and temporal metrics, including Shipping Days, Profit Margin, chronological calendar anchors, and commercial discount tiers.

Exploratory analysis and bivariate correlation modeling (Pearson linear r and Spearman monotonic rho) uncovered critical commercial insights: (1) Volume vs Profit Asymmetry: Technology captures 50.8% of profit ($145.5K) at a 17.4% margin, whereas Furniture yields only 6.4% profit ($18.5K, 2.49% margin) despite generating $742K in sales; (2) The 20% Discount Cliff: Promotional discounting exhibits severe non-linear deterioration above 20% (Spearman rho = -0.5434), driving catastrophic losses down to -$6,599.98; and (3) Regional Disparities: Central region discounting (24.0% average) severely suppresses profitability (7.92% margin) compared to West region efficiency (14.94% margin).

All 16 publication-grade figures specified in assignment requirements were exported at 300 DPI (10 x 6 inches) and synthesized alongside 22 tabular summary assets into a comprehensive, professionally styled Microsoft Word document (Superstore_Data_Cleaning_Preliminary_Analysis.docx). The entire project is packaged into a structured GitHub-ready repository with complete documentation, requirement traceability, and one-command reproducibility.
